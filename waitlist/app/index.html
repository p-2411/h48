How Duet works

  1. Words you know light up.

    Orange: you know it. Blue: you’ve learnt to say it. Green: you’ve learnt to use it. Swipe the subtitles for more Japanese.

  2. Ask about any line.

    Duet explains what was said, and how it was said.

  3. A tutor that listens.

    Say it out loud. Duet hears exactly how it sounded and coaches you through it.

  4. Words stick, without pausing.

    At the end of a scene, or a long pause, answer a quick question.

  5. Five minutes a day.

    A short session made from what you watched, your words climbing, and a streak to keep.

A few things to know.

Which languages can I learn?+

Japanese, Spanish and Korean, from English.

Which sites does it work on?+

Anything you stream: YouTube, Netflix, Crunchyroll and more. Duet is a desktop app, not a browser extension, so it floats over whatever video is on your screen.

Will I actually learn just by watching?+

Yes, because the same words keep coming back, the way they do when you move to a new country. Duet colours the ones you know, asks you about them now and then, and brings them back in a five-minute session each day.

How do I know I’m improving?+

Each episode shows how much of it you understood, counting only words you really know. Your words move from seen, to known, to ones you can say and use, and you move up to harder shows as you master each level.

Is it hard to set up?+

No. Install the app, open a video, and Duet appears over it. There is no browser extension and no flashcard app to connect.

Does it record me?+

Your microphone is only on while you talk to Duet or say a line. While a video plays, Duet keeps the last minute of its sound in memory, never on disk, so it can hear a line you ask about; it only sends that line when you ask.

What do I install?+

A desktop app for Mac and Windows. One shortcut shows or hides it.

How much does it cost?+

Early access is free, with no credit card. We will tell you about pricing before it changes.
